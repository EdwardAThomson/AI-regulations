# Abu Dhabi Law No. 3 of 2024: The AI and Advanced Technology Council (AIATC)

*Research and comparison only, not legal advice. AI-assisted analysis that may contain errors; verify against the primary law (the authoritative text is in Arabic).*

**Document:** Law No. 3 of 2024 (Emirate of Abu Dhabi)
**Issued:** 22 January 2024, by Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan as Ruler of Abu Dhabi
**Issuing body:** Government of Abu Dhabi
**Legal status:** Binding emirate-level constitutive law (establishes a body); not an AI conduct regulation
**Source basis:** Official announcements and legal trackers; the full Arabic legal text was not directly reviewed

---

## Summary

Law No. 3 of 2024 establishes the Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Technology Council (AIATC) as an Abu Dhabi government body with legal personality and financial and administrative independence. It is a constitutive law: it creates and empowers an institution to lead Abu Dhabi's AI and advanced-technology policy, research, infrastructure, and investment. It does not regulate the use of AI, and it imposes no obligations on developers or deployers.

## Key points

- **What it creates.** The AIATC, an independent Abu Dhabi council with full legal, financial, and administrative independence, charged with leading the emirate's AI and advanced-technology agenda.
- **Mandate.** Develop and implement policies, strategies, research programmes, infrastructure, and investments related to AI and advanced technology in Abu Dhabi.
- **Responsibilities and objectives.** Set policies and strategies for AI and advanced technology; develop research programmes and establish research centres with local and global partners; oversee and direct AI projects, infrastructure, and investments; launch initiatives to attract leading companies, talent, and investment; strengthen Abu Dhabi's position as a hub for AI and advanced technology.
- **Leadership.** Chairman Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan; Vice Chairman Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan; members including Khaldoon Khalifa Al Mubarak, Jassem Mohamed Bu Ataba Al Zaabi, Faisal Abdulaziz Al Bannai, and Peng Xiao.
- **Character.** An institutional and industrial-policy instrument: it concentrates authority over AI strategy, research, and investment in a single high-level, well-connected body.

## Observations

### Coverage

This is a constitutive law, so its coverage is institutional rather than substantive. It establishes who leads AI policy in Abu Dhabi and what that body may do; it does not define AI, classify risk, prohibit practices, or set transparency, safety, or oversight requirements. As an entry in an AI governance survey it matters not as a rulebook but as the institutional anchor of Abu Dhabi's approach: governance by an empowered central council that sets strategy and directs investment, rather than by horizontal regulation.

### Feasibility

The model is highly feasible by design. Granting the AIATC financial and administrative independence and staffing it with senior leadership (including figures associated with Abu Dhabi's major technology and investment vehicles) gives it real authority and resources to act. For its actual purpose, marshalling research, infrastructure, and investment, this concentrated, top-down structure is well suited to delivery. What the law does not do, and does not purport to do, is create the machinery (definitions, obligations, enforcement) that substantive AI regulation would require.

### Internal consistency

As an establishment law the instrument is internally coherent: it names the body, grants it independence, and lists its functions. The only thing to flag for a governance reader is the breadth of the mandate ("policies, strategies, regulations") relative to the absence of any conduct rules in the law itself: the council is empowered to make policy and potentially regulation in future, but the law is the empowering shell, not the rules. Any binding AI conduct obligations in Abu Dhabi would come from subsequent instruments the AIATC or other authorities issue, not from this law.

### International alignment

The AIATC fits the industrial-policy and sovereign-AI end of the international spectrum rather than the regulatory end. Where the EU codifies obligations and the UK and Singapore convene and issue voluntary frameworks, Abu Dhabi concentrates strategic direction, research, and capital allocation in a single state council. This is closer in spirit to a national AI mission or industrial-strategy body than to a regulator, and it reflects Abu Dhabi's broader bet on compute, sovereign models, and large-scale investment. It also adds a layer of intra-UAE institutional plurality: a federal AI Office, a Dubai delivery programme, and now an independent Abu Dhabi council, each leading AI in its own domain.

### Gaps

For anyone looking to this law for AI rules, the gaps are total and intentional: no risk tiers, no prohibited or high-risk categories, no developer or deployer obligations, no transparency or human-oversight duties, no penalties, and no individual rights or remedies. These are simply not the function of an establishment law. The open question it leaves is whether the AIATC will, in time, issue substantive AI policy or regulation under its broad mandate, and how that would interact with federal instruments and with the ADGM free zone that also operates within Abu Dhabi.

## Overall assessment

Law No. 3 of 2024 is best understood as the institutional foundation of Abu Dhabi's AI strategy rather than as AI regulation. It creates a powerful, independent, well-resourced council to set policy and direct research, infrastructure, and investment, which is a precise expression of the UAE's state-as-builder, industrial-policy approach to AI. Judged as a governance instrument it is, by design, empty of conduct rules: it confers authority rather than imposing obligations. Its significance for this repository is twofold: it marks Abu Dhabi as a distinct centre of AI authority within the UAE (alongside the federal level, Dubai, and the free zones), and it positions a single body to potentially issue substantive AI policy later. For now, it is an empowering shell, and the binding AI rules in Abu Dhabi remain those of the ADGM free zone rather than anything in this law.
