# UAE: Source Document Inventory

Documents to collect for analysis of the United Arab Emirates' AI governance landscape.

**Status key:** Collected & reviewed | Collected | Needed | Lower priority

The UAE has no single comprehensive AI statute. Its position is a layered mix of:
federal strategy and a voluntary ethics charter; federal laws of general application that
bite on AI (data protection, copyright, cybercrime); emirate-level strategies and bodies
(Dubai, Abu Dhabi); independent financial free-zone regimes (DIFC, ADGM) that have the
most concrete binding AI-adjacent rules; and sector regulators (Central Bank, SCA, health).

## Notes

- The UAE has no standalone, comprehensive, binding AI law. Federal posture is strategy + a
  voluntary 12-principle Charter, layered over generally applicable laws (PDPL, copyright,
  cybercrime).
- The most concrete binding AI-adjacent rule is DIFC Regulation 10 (Sep 2023), one of the
  first instruments in the region to directly regulate autonomous/semi-autonomous systems
  in a data-protection frame. It introduces notices, AI registers, certification, and an
  Autonomous Systems Officer for high-risk processing. Full enforcement from Jan 2026.
- ADGM and DIFC are independent common-law free zones with their own data-protection regimes;
  treat them as distinct sub-jurisdictions, not merely "Dubai" or "Abu Dhabi" law.
- Strong "state-as-adopter" theme: AI minister (2017, world-first), Chief AI Officers across
  government, AI as advisory Cabinet member (from Jan 2026), AI-assisted legislative drafting
  (Regulatory Intelligence Office, 2025). Governance posture leans pro-innovation and
  adoption-led rather than restriction-led.
- Compare-and-contrast candidates within this repo: Singapore and UK (soft-law, pro-innovation,
  no comprehensive statute) and the financial-services cross-jurisdictional draft.
