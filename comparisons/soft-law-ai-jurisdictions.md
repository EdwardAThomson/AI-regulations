# Soft-Law AI Jurisdictions: UK, Singapore, Switzerland, UAE, USA

A top-level map of the jurisdictions in this repo that have **not** enacted a comprehensive,
horizontal AI statute, and instead govern AI through strategy, voluntary frameworks, sector
regulators, and generally applicable law. This is the entry point; it routes down to the
deeper, thesis-specific comparisons.

The single sharpest division in global AI governance is not between these jurisdictions but
between **one binding pole and everyone else**:

- **Binding pole: the EU.** The AI Act is a comprehensive, horizontal, binding statute with risk
  tiers, prohibited practices, a GPAI chapter, and penalties. It is the outlier.
- **Soft-law field: UK, Singapore, Switzerland, UAE, USA.** No comprehensive AI Act. Governance
  is voluntary, sector-led, or rides on existing law.

Five of the repo's six jurisdictions sit in the soft-law field. This document compares them; the
EU appears only as the contrast pole. (For the EU itself, see [USA vs EU](USA-EU-comparison.md)
and [UK vs EU](UK-EU-comparison.md).)

## The field at a glance

| Jurisdiction | Primary soft-law instrument | Binding AI-specific rules? | EU AI Act as compliance floor? | Distinctive character |
|--------------|----------------------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------|
| **UK** | Pro-Innovation White Paper + 5 cross-sectoral principles via regulators | None | Yes (commercial) | Frontier-safety leadership (AISI); compute investment |
| **Singapore** | Model AI Governance Framework stack (2020 → 2024 → 2026) | None | Yes (commercial) + ASEAN | Practical governance tooling (AI Verify); world-first agentic framework |

| **USA** | NIST AI RMF (voluntary) + Executive Orders; Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights | **Yes, at state level** (e.g. Colorado AI Act) | Partial (large self-contained market) | Big Tech home jurisdiction; agency + state-law patchwork |

## What the field shares

1. **No comprehensive AI Act.** All five rejected (or never pursued) horizontal binding AI
   legislation.
2. **Pro-innovation framing.** All position lighter-touch regulation as a competitive advantage
   against the EU model.
3. **Existing law does the work.** Data protection, consumer protection, financial regulation, and
   sector regulators carry most of the load; AI governance extends rather than replaces them.
4. **Voluntary frameworks as soft infrastructure.** Reference frameworks (NIST AI RMF, Singapore's
   Model Framework, the UK principles, the UAE Charter) function as internal scaffolding rather
   than enforceable rules.

---

## The two axes that actually separate them

### Axis 1: Is the soft-law centre uniform, or does it have binding sub-jurisdictions?

This is the most useful distinction the broad view reveals. The field splits in two:

- **Uniformly soft:** **UK, Singapore, Switzerland.** No binding AI-specific obligation anywhere
  in the jurisdiction; governance is voluntary or rides on regulators' general powers.
- **Soft centre, binding sub-jurisdiction:** **UAE and USA.** The federal/national centre is
  soft-law, but a sub-jurisdiction imposes genuinely binding AI rules:
  - **UAE**: the **DIFC** financial free zone (Regulation 10: binding obligations on AI processing
    of personal data, borrowing EU AI Act vocabulary).
  - **USA**: **states** (e.g. the Colorado AI Act's high-risk regime). Federal soft-law sits over a
    growing state-law patchwork.

  In both, the right first question is not "what does the country require?" but "**which
  sub-jurisdiction applies?**"

### Axis 2: How much does the EU AI Act anchor them?

- **High (treaty-adjacent): Switzerland.** Bilateral EU framework makes divergence a market-access
  problem.
- **High (commercial): UK, Singapore.** Firms serving EU users comply with the AI Act regardless of
  domestic law; domestic frameworks sit on top of that floor.
- **Partial: USA.** US firms serving the EU comply, but the US market is large and self-contained
  enough that the EU is not the gravitational anchor; domestic dynamics (states, agencies,
  administration swings) dominate.
- **Low: UAE.** Oriented to Gulf/MENA/Asia/US; references OECD/UNESCO/NIST rather than the EU Act;
  not a CoE Convention signatory. The most genuinely autonomous of the five.

---

## What each is known for (one line)

- **UK**: frontier-safety evaluation (AISI) and compute ambition.
- **Singapore**: practical governance tools (AI Verify) and the first agentic-AI framework.
- **Switzerland**: neutral diplomatic convening; minimal domestic governance.
- **UAE**: aggressive state adoption and industrial policy, with a binding free-zone overlay.
- **USA**: scale and market power; soft federally, fragmenting into state law.
